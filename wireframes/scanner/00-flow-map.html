FLOW: first-class barcode scanning in the /m phone shell
PERSONA: Marvin, phone in one hand and a product in the other — kitchen counter or supermarket aisle. Arrived wanting it logged without typing anything.
JOB: barcode → confirmed product → today's food log, in under ~10 seconds.
ENTRY: scan FAB in the /m tab bar (always one tap away); scan button in the Log tab capture row; later: Android app shortcut + native home tile deep-linking /m/scan.
EXIT: entry sits in today's ledger (editable there like any other entry). Either keep scanning the next item or tap Done back to Log.
VIEWPORT: mobile 390 — this flow is phone-only by nature. The desktop /nutrition/scan page stays as-is for the desk case.
JOURNEYtap a step
01 — Entry points
Where scanning lives in the shell: tab-bar FAB + Log tab capture row.
02 — Scanner
Full-bleed live viewfinder, torch, fallbacks (photo / type digits), permission-denied state.
03 — Preview
Resolved product as a bottom sheet: portion, macros vs day, one Log action. Unknown-barcode state.
04 — Logged
Stay-and-scan loop with running day total; Done exits to the ledger.
05 — Usuals
The zeroth entry point: your frequent cluster as one-tap chips — no camera, no typing. Tally on repeat taps.
Decisions taken (challenge any):
— FAB goes straight to the scanner, not to a capture sheet. Text capture already has two homes (Log input, Telegram → /food); scanning is the one capture only the phone can do, so the phone's biggest affordance belongs to it. Long-press the FAB is reserved for a future capture sheet (photo-of-meal, voice…).
— After logging you stay in the scanner ("scan another"), because the multi-item moment (unpacking a shop, meal from 3 products) is the power case. Done exits to Log.
— The flow reuses the existing decode → resolve → confirm pipeline from /nutrition/scan; wireframes change where it lives and what it leads with, not the plumbing.
— Capture is a ladder, cheapest rung first: tap a usual (known item, zero decisions) → scan (packaged, unknown) → type (everything else, LLM estimates). The real frequent-foods data justifies the top rung: pale ale 48×, Guinness 32×, a granola family, all with known macros — logging those should cost one tap, not a camera.
